# 0012 - Extracting what is actually being sold: lots, defects and completeness

**Context:** Stage 3 gives two ways to answer "which product is this", and measurement shows both are blind to the same thing. `epid` (`0006`) is eBay's catalog id and identifies the product model exactly: 47 listings share epid `10048191653`, from 38 distinct sellers with 47 distinct photos, and they really are all the HEG-001 Switch OLED. CLIP embeddings (`0009`) retrieve visual similarity. **Neither can see what is in the box.** Within that single epid sit a bare "Tablet Only" and a "Tablet Charge And Dock And HDMI Cord" bundle, both listed at $159.99, across a total range of $127.99 to $255.00. The same failure appeared end to end: a captured Depop listing reading "Switch OLED barely used **w/ box**" matched six eBay listings that were all "**TABLET ONLY**", at a price spread of 1.04 and a confidence of 1.00. Tight, confident, and the wrong comparison. Three distinct hazards were then measured across the 12,678-listing eBay corpus, and they are not equally important. **Multi-item lots are the severe one:** a "Lot of 50 SK Hynix 64GB" sits at **$113,000** inside the same `Memory (RAM)` category as single sticks, and a "*LOT OF 2* RTX 3090" at $2,800 against a single card's several hundred. Excluding the 24 lot listings from that category (2% of it) drops the mean by 28% and the maximum from $113,000 to $21,936. **Defects are the largest by count:** among graphics cards, listings flagged "for parts / not working / cracked / as-is" have a median of **$151.08** against **$420.00** for clean ones, a 64% gap, on 268 of 2,544 listings. **Completeness is the subtlest and behaves exactly as expected:** among consoles, bare-unit listings median $129.99, unstated $174.99, and with-extras $190.00. All three signals live in free-text titles. eBay's structured `localizedAspects` does not carry any of them: the common keys are Brand, Model, Color, MPN and Storage Capacity, and there is no "what's included" aspect at all.

**Decision:** Extract three variant properties from the title (with `aspects` consulted where it helps) and store them on `Listing`: `lot_size` (`int | None`, where `None` means a single item), `completeness` (`"bare" | "complete" | "bundle" | None`, where `None` means unstated rather than average), and `has_defect` (`bool`). A `variant_signals` JSON column keeps the matched tokens, mirroring what `sale_signals` does for sale confidence, so no classification is opaque and every rule stays separately falsifiable against real titles. Comp selection then treats these as **filters, not weights**: a lot listing and a for-parts listing are excluded from a comp set outright rather than discounted, because they are not noisy measurements of the right quantity, they are measurements of a different quantity. Completeness filters only when the query listing's own completeness is known, since forcing a match on an unstated field would discard most of the corpus. Extraction is rule-based (regexes over a curated vocabulary), not a model.

**Alternatives considered:** Dividing a lot's price by its size to recover a unit price was rejected, and it is the tempting one. Bulk pricing is not linear (a lot of 50 sells at a discount per unit precisely because it is a lot), and the units within a lot are frequently not identical: "LOT OF 54 SAMSUNG HYNIX MICRON 16GB DDR4" is three manufacturers in one listing. A derived unit price would be a plausible-looking number with unknown error, which is worse than an excluded row. Discounting defect listings by a multiplier instead of excluding them was rejected on the same reasoning that `0007` used to split sale confidence from price confidence: "this item is damaged" is not a statement about measurement uncertainty, it is a different product condition, and averaging it into a comp set for working units produces a number describing neither. Training a classifier was rejected as premature and probably permanently wrong-shaped: the signals are sparse (0.6% to 4.2% of titles each), the vocabulary is small and stable, there are no labels, and a regex that fires on "for parts" is auditable in a way a model's decision boundary is not. Doing this at query time rather than storing it was rejected because comp selection has to filter in SQL, and recomputing across the corpus per query is wasted work on a property that changes only when a title does. Waiting for more sold data before building this was rejected because it is the wrong order: comparability has to come first, or stage 4 gets tuned against comp sets that mix bare units with bundles and there is no way to tell a scoring bug from a comp-set bug.

**Consequences:** Four columns and migration `0012`. Extraction is deliberately **high-precision and low-recall**: most listings state nothing about completeness and will stay `None`, which is the honest answer and must not be read as "complete". That means this improves the comp sets it touches and leaves the majority untouched, which is the right trade when the alternative is guessing. The rules are English-and-eBay-specific and will need extending as new categories arrive, so `variant_signals` exists partly to make that maintenance possible: a listing classified oddly can be traced to the token that did it. The lot exclusion is the highest-value part by a wide margin and the least likely to be controversial. Two known gaps are recorded rather than solved: "READ" in a title (305 listings) is a strong seller-side signal that something is wrong but says nothing about *what*, so it is captured as a signal without changing classification; and negations like "no charger" or "without dock" (425 listings) are captured but not yet used to downgrade completeness, because getting negation scope right in title-case fragments is a genuinely harder problem than the rest of this and does not need solving to capture most of the value. Finally, this sharpens what stage 3b is actually for. It began as "NLP extraction of brand/model/size", and brand and model turn out to be largely handled already by `epid` and `localizedAspects`; the part no structured field provides, and the part that moves price most, is what is in the box and whether it works.
