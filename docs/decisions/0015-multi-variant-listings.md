# 0015 - Multi-variant listings, where the price and the title describe different items

> Migration number and ADR number differ here (migration `0014`, ADR `0015`).
> They are independent sequences, and ADR `0014` (valuation) needed no schema
> change because valuation is computed on read.

**Context:** The first real deal scan after `0014` returned a ranked list of apparent bargains, and one of them was "Apple iPhone 14 128GB 256GB - Unlocked AT&T Verizon T-Mobile - All Colors" at $259.99 against a $650 estimate, a 60% discount. It is not a discount. eBay lets one listing offer several configurations, and it displays the price of the **cheapest** one, so the $259.99 is the entry-level variant while the title names all of them and the k-NN match picked up comps for the larger ones. The price and the title describe different items. Measured across the corpus, 671 listings (5.0%) are like this, and they concentrate exactly where they do most damage: at the top of a deal ranking, because a from-price beside a full-spec title produces the largest apparent discounts in the dataset. This is the same shape as the accessory problem `0013` found, discovered the same way (reading the top of the ranking), and it is the second confirmation that the deal ranking is the most effective audit available for everything upstream of it.

**Decision:** Detect multi-variant listings and set `price_is_from`, joining lots, defects and accessories in `usable_as_comp`, so they are excluded both from comp sets and from deal scanning. Three rules, in precedence order. **Explicit variant wording** ("all colors", "choose your storage", "pick color & size", "your choice") is conclusive in any category and covers 373 listings. **Three or more distinct capacities** in one title is a variant list, since no single product has three storage figures, except in the categories where it does. **Two capacities** is conclusive only in categories with a single capacity dimension (phones, consoles, tablets), where "128GB 256GB" cannot mean anything else. Capacities are normalized to GB before counting, so "256/512GB/1TB" reads as three.

**Alternatives considered:** Rewriting `capacity_gb` to the *smallest* capacity rather than the largest, so price and spec agree, was the tempting fix and is wrong: the listing still offers the other variants, the buyer may not get the cheap one, and it would silently convert a listing that cannot be compared into one that looks like it can. Trusting eBay to tell us was investigated first and there is nothing to trust: the Browse API's `itemSummary` gives one `price` with no indication that variants exist, and `price.value` is simply the lowest. (`getItem` does return more detail for some multi-variant listings, but the disappearance check only touches a small budgeted subset, so it cannot be the primary signal.) Excluding by *category* was rejected because multi-variant listings appear across phones, consoles and drives alike. Applying the capacity-count rule everywhere was rejected on measurement: it fired on 191 real machines, because "Gaming PC Ryzen 5 7600X 16GB RAM 512GB SSD RTX 4060 8GB" is three capacities describing three different components. Gating that rule out of multi-component categories fixed it exactly.

**Consequences:** Migration `0014` adds one indexed boolean, and 671 listings leave the comp pool and the deal scan, which is 5.0% of the corpus on top of the 9.2% already excluded by `0012` and `0013`. The rule is deliberately conservative in the one place it could easily overreach: two capacities outside a phone or console category is left alone, so "Intel 32GB/1024GB NVMe M.2 SSD" (an Optane hybrid, not a choice) stays a valid comp. That does mean genuine two-variant listings in component categories are missed; the three-or-more rule catches the ones that matter most, and a wrongly excluded listing costs one comp while a wrongly included one produces a fake bargain at the top of the feed. Worth recording for later: `price_is_from` currently means "do not use this listing at all", and a richer treatment would keep it as a valid comp for its *cheapest* variant once eBay's variant detail is available for a listing, which the `getItem` body sometimes carries. That is deferred rather than rejected. Finally, this is the third correctness fix in a row surfaced by ranking rather than by testing, and the pattern is now explicit in `CLAUDE.md`: after any change to extraction or valuation, run `find_deals` and read the top of the list before trusting anything.
