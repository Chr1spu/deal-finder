# 0013 - Spec extraction, and the accessories that were pretending to be products

**Context:** `0012` made comp sets *cleaner* by excluding lots and defects, and measurement afterwards showed it had not made them *comparable*. Residual price spread inside a filtered comp set stayed at a median of 4.3x, and far worse in the categories built out of interchangeable-looking parts: **Memory (RAM) 15.0x** and **Graphics/Video Cards 8.0x**. The cause is the one `0009` already identified and could not fix from the image side: every DDR4 stick looks identical whether it is 16GB or 64GB, so visual similarity carries almost no information about price for commodity components. Two separate problems turned out to be hiding under that one number, and they need different fixes.

**The first is spec, and the data says exactly where it lives.** Capacity is present in the title but *absent from eBay's structured aspects* precisely where it matters most: RAM has capacity in 97% of titles and 12% of aspects, graphics cards in 79% of titles and **0.3%** of aspects. Cell phones, which do not have this problem, have 99% aspect coverage. Capacity alone separates RAM medians cleanly ($59.50 / $75.00 / $181.13 / $799.99 / $1,975.00 for 8/16/32/64/128GB) but leaves 30-80x spread *within* each capacity. Adding generation and form factor collapses it: 32GB RAM goes from **82.7x** overall to 2.8x (DDR4 laptop), 4.4x (DDR4 desktop), 2.7x (DDR4 server), 3.8x (DDR5 laptop) and 3.7x (DDR5 server). Graphics cards behave differently again: capacity is secondary and the *chipset* is the price, so extracting a model key produces a clean ladder (rtx-4090 $2,775, rtx-5080 $1,649, rtx-4080-super $1,194, rtx-4070-super $652).

**The second problem was invisible until the model key exposed it, and it is worse.** Grouping graphics cards by chipset gave `rtx-3090` a spread of **1428x**. Inspecting the low end explains it, and none of it is a pricing anomaly: `$6.61` "Quick start guide and support guide books ONLY", `$34.99` "Backplate Replacement for ASUS ROG STRIX RTX 3090", `$50.00` "original box include inside box. No graphics card", `$88.00` "3-Fan Heatsink Cooler Assembly", `$100.00` "Heatsink ONLY", `$187.00` "NVLink Bridge for RTX 3090", `$199.95` "CASE for GIGABYTE AORUS RTX 3090". **These are accessories *for* the product, not the product.** They match on model string and on image (a photo of a GPU cooler looks like a GPU), so both identity mechanisms accept them, and they sit at 2-20% of the real price, which drags any median or mean far below reality. Measured across the corpus, an accessory noun together with "for" appears in 1.0% of listings, and every sampled instance was genuine. A related class of 0.2% is non-functional hardware described as "no display" or "no power", which the `0012` defect vocabulary missed.

**Decision:** Extend title extraction with five fields, all indexed because comp selection filters on them in SQL. `is_accessory` (bool): the listing is a part, replacement or accessory *for* a product rather than the product, detected by an accessory-noun vocabulary in combination with "for", "only", or an explicit "no graphics card / no GPU". Excluded from comp sets outright, joining lots and defects in `usable_as_comp`. `capacity_gb` (int): normalized to gigabytes so 1TB and 1024GB compare, taking the largest capacity mentioned since titles list several. `spec_generation` (str): DDR3/4/5 and PCIe Gen3/4/5. `form_factor` (str): laptop / desktop / server for memory, m.2 / 2.5in for drives. `model_key` (str): a normalized chipset identifier for graphics cards and processors, where the model *is* the spec. The `0012` defect vocabulary also gains "no display / no power / no boot / does not post". Comp filtering applies these the same way `0012` applies completeness: match when the query listing states a value, keep unstated rows, drop stated mismatches.

**Alternatives considered:** A single JSON `specs` column was rejected because every one of these is a comp filter and filtering in SQL on JSON keys is both slower and much easier to get subtly wrong than a typed indexed column. The project's existing convention already draws that line (typed for what is queried, JSON for what is not, per `0006`), and `variant_signals` continues to carry the audit trail. Category-specific tables were rejected as far too much structure for five fields, and they would force a join into the hot comp query. Deriving specs from `localizedAspects` instead of titles was rejected on the measurement above: aspects are missing in exactly the two categories that need this most (0.3% on graphics cards), because eBay's per-category aspect sets are richest for consumer goods and thinnest for components. Aspects are still consulted as a fallback where present. Treating accessories as merely low-confidence comps rather than excluding them was rejected for the same reason `0012` gave for lots: a heatsink is not a noisy measurement of a graphics card's value, it is a different product, and no weight makes it informative. Extending this to a learned model was rejected again, and more confidently than in `0012`: the accessory rule was found, validated and understood in an afternoon by reading real titles, and its failure modes are inspectable.

**Consequences:** Migration `0013`, five columns, and `ml/extract.py` grows from three rules to eight. That file is now large enough that its vocabulary lists are the maintenance surface, which is what `variant_signals` exists to make tractable. The extraction stays high-precision and low-recall by design, so most listings still carry no spec at all and are neither helped nor harmed. Two limits are recorded rather than solved. DDR5 desktop memory keeps a 19.1x spread even fully segmented, most likely single sticks against multi-stick kits, which needs a quantity-within-listing concept distinct from `lot_size` and is deliberately deferred. And `model_key` is currently GPU-and-CPU shaped; extending it to other categories means more vocabulary, not a different design, but it should be driven by a measured spread problem rather than added speculatively. Finally, the accessory finding is a reminder worth keeping: it was invisible in aggregate, invisible to `epid`, invisible to CLIP, and became obvious the moment listings were grouped by a key that should have made them comparable and did not. Grouping by something that *ought* to produce agreement, then reading the disagreements, is a cheap and repeatable way to find this class of problem.
